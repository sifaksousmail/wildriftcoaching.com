Wild Rift Target Selection Guide: Who to Hit in Fights

Free guide by Danny · Target selection · 7 min read · Published September 6, 2026

“Focus their carry” sounds clear until the enemy carry is behind a tank, two defensive abilities, and a wall. Good Wild Rift target selection starts with access: which enemy can you affect from here without walking into a losing exchange?

A target can be important and still be unreachable. Your job is to recognize the opening that makes the important target available, then change targets when the fight changes.

Quick answer: choose a reachable target with a purpose

Before committing, ask three questions: can I reach this enemy, what can punish me, and what does attacking them achieve? Sometimes the answer is sustained damage into the front line. Sometimes it is a short crowd-control spell on a diver. Sometimes it is waiting until a carry spends their escape.

1. Separate priority from access

Priority describes who matters most if you can affect them. Access describes whether your champion can reach them now and what it costs. A fed marksman may be the highest priority, but diving through the enemy team while your allies are out of range can remove your own damage from the fight.

Imagine two enemies in front of you: a tank walking toward your team and a low-health mage behind them. The mage is attractive, but reaching them would require your only movement ability. If the tank can stop you afterward, the chase is not a clean opening. Damage the accessible target, move sideways with your team, and watch for the mage to step into range.

Use the win condition guide to identify which enemy matters most. During combat, add the access check before acting on that priority.

2. Hit the front line while preserving your position

For an ADC, attacking a tank can be the correct choice when it lets you deal damage without crossing enemy engage range.

This is an active decision. Move between attacks, watch the angle of the enemy back line, and stop stepping forward when doing so exposes you. If a fragile enemy enters safe range, switch. If the tank retreats into a position your team cannot follow, do not automatically chase them either.

There is also a difference between basic attacks and major abilities. You may keep attacking the front line while holding a long cooldown for the enemy who is about to dive you. The ADC positioning guide explains the spacing that keeps these options open.

3. Name the cooldown that blocks your target

“I cannot go in yet” becomes more useful when you can name why. It might be a stun held by the support, the carry's escape, or a defensive ability that would absorb your main burst. Watch that specific tool instead of trying to track every spell in the fight.

Suppose you are playing a melee damage dealer and the enemy support still has the crowd control needed to stop your entry. Stay close enough to respond while keeping out of their easy range. If they spend it on your front line, check again: is the carry reachable, can your allies follow, and do you have a way to survive the return damage?

One used spell is evidence of an opening, not proof. Another enemy may still cover the target. Include other champions who can punish the route.

4. Switch targets when the reason changes

Locking onto one enemy mentally can be as costly as selecting the wrong enemy mechanically. A carry who was exposed can retreat behind protection. A tank you were damaging can leave range. A diver can suddenly become the closest threat to your team's damage dealer.

  • A diver reaches your carry: turn damage or control onto the diver if it helps your carry survive.
  • Your target becomes untargetable or otherwise unavailable: reassess the other reachable enemies instead of spending the entire window moving after them.
  • A protected carry steps forward: switch when you can punish the mistake without crossing the same danger that blocked you before.
  • A fleeing enemy pulls you away from the team: stop and check whether the chase leaves a more important threat unanswered.

Recheck before spending mobility. A basic attack is a small commitment; dashing after a target can remove your way back.

5. As a support, choose who needs to be stopped

Your best target may be the enemy about to deal damage. If a diver is approaching your fed ADC, holding crowd control for that entry can provide more value than using it on a distant tank.

For example, your team starts dealing damage to an enemy frontliner while an assassin moves along the side. Position so you can answer the assassin's approach. Do not spend every defensive tool on the frontliner simply because they arrived first. Once the assassin commits, stop them long enough for your carry to move and attack.

If your team instead catches an exposed carry with allies close enough to follow, an engage can be useful. Decide what protection you give up when you commit. The support macro guide connects that choice to your wider role.

6. Give melee and assassin entries an exit condition

Melee champions often need a clearer entry window because walking into damage range also puts them in enemy control range. Assassins should watch for isolated or exposed targets, but a low health bar alone does not make an entry safe.

Before going in, name both the trigger and the exit: “I enter if their carry separates after using escape; I leave when their support turns.” If the trigger never appears, threaten the angle without forcing an entry.

A bruiser may instead stay with the team and pressure the nearest threat. Do not copy an assassin's target plan onto a champion that needs a longer exchange.

7. Make the controls follow your decision

Riot's documented targeting update describes targeting priority and target-lock filtering. Test the controls available in your current client in Practice, especially how you select, release, and change targets. An automatic priority setting cannot judge whether chasing an enemy is a good decision.

One replay drill: explain three target changes

Pause before a fight and name your first useful target plus the spell that could punish you. Then review three moments where access changed. Record what happened, whether you changed targets, and the cost of staying committed. For your next games, use one short cue: “New threat, new target check.”

Target selection FAQ

Who should I focus in a Wild Rift teamfight?

Choose a target you can affect safely and meaningfully from your current position. Reach an exposed carry when the opening exists; damage the front line or stop a diver when the carry is protected.

Should an ADC attack the tank in Wild Rift?

Yes, when the tank is the useful target within safe range. Reposition between attacks and watch for access to a more vulnerable enemy. Do not cross available enemy crowd control just to reach the back line.

When should I switch targets in a fight?

Switch when a new threat reaches your carry, your current target becomes unsafe or unavailable, or a more valuable enemy becomes safely reachable. Reassess before spending a mobility or crowd-control ability.

Should supports always engage on the enemy carry?

No. If an enemy diver can reach your main damage dealer, holding crowd control to stop that entry can be more useful. Engage when your team can follow and the opening is worth the protection you give up.
