Wild Rift VOD Review Checklist

Free guide by Danny · VOD review · 8 min read

A Wild Rift VOD review checklist keeps replay review useful. Without a checklist, most players watch the whole game, remember the last fight, blame one teammate, and queue again without changing the habit that caused the loss.

The goal is smaller and stronger: review the right moments, find one repeated pattern, and turn that pattern into one rule for the next ranked block.

Quick answer: review setup before outcome

For each replay, check your goal, first death, first recall, first objective setup, first lost fight, and the moment tilt or autopilot changed your decision quality. The useful mistake usually starts before the death or lost objective appears on screen.

If you want Danny to review the replay with you, send the same notes with your rank, role, champion, timestamp, and one question. That makes a Wild Rift VOD review sharper from the first pause.

Before pressing play: write one question

Start the review with one question. Why was I late to dragon? Why did my lead disappear? Why did I die before Baron? Why did I keep fighting with no wave? Why did I stop checking the map after one bad fight?

One question protects the review from becoming a random list of mistakes. If the replay shows a bigger problem, you can adjust, but the first question gives the session a clean direction.

Ranked routine

Checklist 1: deaths

Pause ten to thirty seconds before each important death. Do not only ask who killed you. Ask why you were there, what information was missing, and whether your wave, vision, recall, or objective timer made the death likely.

  • Was the danger visible before the death?
  • Did I walk into fog without a reason?
  • Was my wave safe enough for this move?
  • Was the fight connected to an objective, tower, wave, or reset?
  • Did I spend a summoner spell because I arrived late, not because the fight was good?

Checklist 2: recalls and tempo

Bad recalls quietly ruin Wild Rift games. A late base before dragon can become weak vision, low health, no item spike, a lost fight, and then a snowball that looks like a team problem.

Review your first two recalls and every recall before a major objective. If you often leave base after the objective has already started, use the Wild Rift recall timing guide as the next practice page.

  • Did I recall with enough time to return before the objective?
  • Did I stay for one extra wave or camp and lose tempo?
  • Did I buy before the fight or fight with unspent gold?
  • Did my reset help my team set vision, push mid, or hold side wave?

Checklist 3: waves and objective setup

Objective review should start before the fight. Check whether mid wave, side wave, vision, health, cooldowns, and role positions made the contest realistic. If your team arrived late with no push and no vision, the mistake was not only the smite, engage, or final skillshot.

For a deeper objective framework, connect this checklist with the Wild Rift objective control guide and vision control guide.

  • Was mid pushed before dragon, Herald, Baron, or Elder?
  • Did we set or deny vision before face-checking river?
  • Were we grouped too early, too late, or in the wrong lane?
  • Was trading the objective better than forcing a late contest?

Checklist 4: fights and role job

When reviewing a lost fight, pause before the engage. Ask what your champion was supposed to do in that fight. ADC spacing, support peel, jungle entry timing, mid cooldowns, Baron lane flank pressure, and front-to-back patience are different jobs.

If you cannot describe your job in one sentence, the fight will look random. Start with role coaching or the role-specific macro guides, then come back to the replay.

  • What was my champion's job before the fight started?
  • Did I enter before key cooldowns were used?
  • Did I chase damage instead of protecting the win condition?
  • Did the fight start with numbers, waves, vision, or item advantage?

Checklist 5: tilt and autopilot

Replay review should include mental state because tilt changes decisions. Look for the first moment where you stopped playing the map and started playing the emotion: revenge fights, rushed recalls, angry pings, instant re-queue energy, or champion swaps after one loss.

If this pattern repeats, pair the replay with the tilt guide, autopilot guide, and losing streak guide.

End with one ranked rule

The review is not finished until it changes the next block. Write one rule that is small enough to remember in-game. Examples: reset before dragon when I have high gold, do not enter river without mid push, review first death after each block, or stop after two emotional losses.

Wild Rift VOD review checklist FAQ

What should I check first in a Wild Rift VOD review?

Start with one ranked question, then review the thirty seconds before your first death, first objective setup, and first lost fight. Those moments usually show the real habit.

How many mistakes should I write down from one replay?

Write one main repeated mistake and one practice rule for the next ranked block. A short note you use is better than a long list you forget.

Can I use this checklist before coaching?

Yes. The checklist helps you send Danny a clearer replay, timestamp, rank, role, champion, and question before a VOD review or coaching session.

Is VOD review better than buying a Wild Rift boost?

VOD review is safer for long-term improvement because it teaches your own decisions. Danny offers coaching, replay review, and self-study resources, not account boosting.
